Key-Agnostic Neural Cryptanalysis of Chaos-Based Image Encryption Under Unseen Keys
Core idea

Train a neural network on plaintext–ciphertext image pairs generated using multiple secret keys, then investigate whether the network can learn the underlying structural weaknesses of a chaos-based encryption scheme and reconstruct plaintext images from ciphertexts generated with completely unseen keys.

The important novelty is not merely neural decryption.

The thesis asks:

Does a deep-learning model learn the encryption transformation itself, rather than memorizing a particular secret key?

That gives you a much stronger master's research problem.

2. Why this is substantially better than the attached idea

The attached proposal says essentially:

plaintext + ciphertext pairs → train CNN → learn mapping → reconstruct unseen ciphertext.

That is already very close to the 2019 work, which demonstrated an end-to-end, key-independent deep-learning attack against chaos-based image encryption and tested static/dynamic key scenarios.

And a 2025 publication has gone further by applying a DL known-plaintext attack to three chaos-based encryption algorithms using CNN/U-Net-style approaches.

Therefore, your thesis should move from:

"Can CNN break chaos encryption?"

to:

"Under what conditions can neural cryptanalysis generalize across unseen secret keys and different encryption configurations?"

That is a much better research question.

4. Research problem

Chaos-based image encryption schemes often rely on chaotic maps to produce permutation/diffusion sequences that obscure image structure.

The fundamental question is whether these transformations are genuinely resistant to data-driven cryptanalysis.

Instead of explicitly estimating the secret key, your proposed framework treats the encryption algorithm as a black-box transformation:

$$ C = E_K(P) $$

where:

\(P\) = plaintext image
\(K\) = secret key
\(E_K\) = chaos-based encryption
\(C\) = ciphertext

Your neural attacker learns an approximation:

$$ \hat P = F_\theta(C) $$

The critical experiment is:

$$ K_{train} \cap K_{test} = \emptyset $$

Therefore:

The model has never seen the test keys during training.

If:

$$ \hat P \approx P $$

for unseen keys, that demonstrates cross-key cryptanalytic generalization.

7. Encryption methodology

I recommend implementing one primary chaos encryption algorithm yourself, rather than depending on someone else's implementation.

For example:

$$ x_{n+1}=r x_n(1-x_n) $$

$$ x_0 \in (0,1), \qquad r \approx 4 $$

Generate a chaotic sequence.

Then implement:

Rearrange pixels according to the chaotic sequence.

8. VERY IMPORTANT: don't call the neural network "decryption"

Scientifically, I would describe it as:

neural cryptanalysis / learned ciphertext-to-plaintext reconstruction

rather than conventional decryption.

Because your model does not necessarily recover the cryptographic key.

It learns an approximation:

$$ F_\theta(C) \rightarrow P $$

This distinction will make your thesis much more defensible.

17. Loss function

I would not rely solely on MSE.

Use a combined reconstruction objective:

$$ L = \lambda_1 L_{MSE} + \lambda_2 L_{SSIM} + \lambda_3 L_{perceptual} $$

where:

MSE → pixel accuracy
SSIM → structural similarity
perceptual loss → visual feature similarity

For your master's thesis, this gives you a strong methodological component.

19. Your most important metric

I would introduce:

Key Generalization Gap (KGG)

Define:

$$ KGG = Performance_{seen\ keys} - Performance_{unseen\ keys} $$

For example:

PSNR

Seen keys       = 31.2 dB
Unseen keys     = 26.8 dB

KGG             = 4.4 dB

This gives your thesis a clean quantitative way to answer:

How much does neural cryptanalysis degrade when the attacker encounters an unseen key?

That is much more interesting than simply reporting PSNR.

21. Your research questions

I recommend exactly these three RQs.

RQ1

Can a deep neural network learn to reconstruct plaintext images from ciphertexts produced by a chaos-based image encryption scheme without explicitly recovering the secret key?

RQ2

How effectively does the learned cryptanalytic model generalize to ciphertexts generated using previously unseen secret keys?

RQ3

How does the effectiveness of neural cryptanalysis change across different chaos maps, image distributions, and amounts of known plaintext-ciphertext data?

These are strong master's-level questions.

22. Research hypotheses
H1

A deep neural network can reconstruct meaningful approximations of plaintext images from chaos-based ciphertexts.

H2

A model trained across diverse keys can generalize to unseen keys, indicating that it has learned structural properties of the encryption transformation rather than merely memorizing keys.

H3

Attack performance decreases as the cryptosystem's configuration changes, providing a measurable estimate of the attack's transferability.
